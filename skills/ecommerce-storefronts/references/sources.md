# Sources

Accessed 12 August 2026. This package is a broad evidence landscape, not a systematic review.
Official standards, platform documentation, and regulators establish current contracts within their
scope. Field experiments and academic studies support bounded causal findings. Baymard's moderated
usability research and cross-site benchmarks expose recurring failure modes; implementation
prevalence and participant observations are not universal conversion effects.

No source establishes a universal best ecommerce layout, category depth, grid count, checkout
length, CVR, promotion, scarcity tactic, or guaranteed sales lift. Apply the defaults here to the
shop's assortment and operating contract, then validate them with local tasks and retained-value
outcomes.

## Research method and storefront discovery

- [Baymard Institute: Research methodology](https://baymard.com/research/methodology) describes its
  published program of 25 qualitative studies, more than 4,400 participant/site sessions, 54
  benchmark rounds, and 335 major sites. It supports using Baymard findings as recurring usability
  evidence while keeping vendor, sample, and benchmark limits visible.
- [Baymard: Homepage and category usability](https://baymard.com/research/homepage-and-category-usability)
  reports testing across 19 sites and eight verticals with later cross-site benchmarking. It supports
  making assortment breadth and category routes understandable, not a fixed homepage template.
- [Baymard: Inferring the product catalog from the homepage](https://baymard.com/blog/inferring-product-catalog-from-homepage)
  reports observed assortment misinterpretation when a homepage overrepresents a narrow campaign.
  Its numeric product-type heuristic is not encoded as a universal quota.
- [Baymard: Homepage carousel](https://baymard.com/blog/homepage-carousel) reports banner blindness,
  missed later slides, and conflicts between interaction and automatic rotation. It supports never
  making a rotating slide the only important route; it does not prove static modules always lift
  revenue.
- [Baymard: Ecommerce navigation best practices](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice),
  [top-level mobile categories](https://baymard.com/blog/main-navigation-product-categories), and
  [mobile “View All”](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-main-nav-view-all) support distinct product
  taxonomy, useful parent destinations, and explicit broad-category links. Exact depth remains
  catalog-specific.
- [Baymard: Avoid overcategorization](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-over-categorization)
  documents comparison failures when shared attributes become separate categories. It supports the
  category-versus-filter good/bad pair.

## Search, lists, and filters

- [Baymard: Ecommerce search usability](https://baymard.com/research/eCommerce-search) and
  [ecommerce search query types](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-query-types) cover 19
  tested sites across eight verticals and later large-site benchmarks. They support query coverage,
  synonym and compatibility handling, and a local evaluation corpus—not identical search logic for
  every store.
- [Baymard: Autocomplete design](https://baymard.com/blog/autocomplete-design) reports scanning and
  choice problems with long or ambiguous suggestion sets. Its suggestion counts are heuristics;
  relevance and viewport determine the local limit.
- [W3C ARIA Authoring Practices: Combobox pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/combobox/)
  establishes keyboard and focus expectations for an editable combobox. ARIA does not repair broken
  semantics or result behavior.
- [Baymard: No-results pages](https://baymard.com/blog/no-results-page) reports ignored generic tips
  and dead-end recovery. It supports corrected queries, adjacent scopes, and helpful alternatives
  without relabeling unrelated products as matches.
- [Baymard: Ecommerce product lists](https://baymard.com/research/ecommerce-product-lists),
  [product-list information](https://baymard.com/blog/product-listing-information), and
  [list-item design](https://baymard.com/blog/list-item-design-ecommerce) support consistent cards
  with category-specific comparison information. More fields are not automatically better.
- [Baymard: Current state of product-list and filtering UX](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-product-list-and-filtering),
  [essential filter types](https://baymard.com/blog/5-essential-filters), and
  [applied filters](https://baymard.com/blog/how-to-design-applied-filters) support grouping ordinary
  variants, category-specific facets, and persistent removable filter state. Benchmark percentages
  are not lift estimates.
- [Liu and Toubia, “Designing Online Product Filtering Systems”](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5052927.pdf?abstractid=5052927&mirid=1)
  reports a randomized 1,200-participant custom-headphone storefront experiment in which filter
  availability changed browsing and choices. It establishes that facets are choice architecture;
  its simulated single-category results do not set a production default.
- [Cai and Xu, “Designing Product Lists for E-Commerce”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10447310802335730)
  reports a controlled study where sort order shifted perceived attribute importance and
  consideration. Its older, bounded setting supports labeling and testing sort policy, not one best
  default.

## Product, price, inventory, and proof

- [Google Merchant Center: Landing-page requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/4752265?hl=en),
  [price mismatch guidance](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9773429?hl=en),
  [sale price](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324471?hl=en), and
  [availability](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324448?hl=en) require the advertised
  product, selected variant, price, currency, and availability to agree through the purchase path
  when Merchant Center is used. This provider contract supports the more general offer-continuity
  default but is not causal conversion evidence.
- [Baymard: Product-page UX](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux),
  [size buttons](https://baymard.com/blog/use-buttons-for-size-selection),
  [price discounts](https://baymard.com/blog/product-page-price-discounts), and
  [out-of-stock products](https://baymard.com/blog/handling-out-of-stock-products) document recurring
  uncertainty around variants, discounts, availability, and dead ends. Controls still need to match
  option count and product type.
- [Baymard: Product image resolution and zoom](https://baymard.com/blog/ensure-sufficient-image-resolution-and-zoom),
  [in-scale images](https://baymard.com/blog/in-scale-product-images), and
  [gallery thumbnails](https://baymard.com/blog/truncating-product-gallery-thumbnails) support useful
  detail, scale, and discoverability. Participant percentages describe Baymard's tests; responsive
  performance remains a guardrail.
- [Cheng, Shao, and Zhang, “The impact of product videos on consumer purchase intention”](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.812579/full)
  reports three pretests and two scenario experiments on two products; usage videos increased
  self-reported intention through diagnosticity and imagery. Its student-heavy sample and intention
  outcome do not justify a universal video-uplift claim.
- [Baymard: Rating distributions](https://baymard.com/blog/user-ratings-distribution-summary) and
  [responding to negative reviews](https://baymard.com/blog/respond-to-negative-user-reviews)
  document shoppers seeking distributions and unfavorable reviews. They support transparent review
  presentation, not a minimum review count.
- [FTC: Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
  explains the U.S. rule effective 21 October 2024 covering fake reviews, conditioned incentives,
  and insider relationships. Current jurisdiction-specific legal review remains necessary.
- [Hong et al., “Understanding the determinants of online review helpfulness”](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Understanding-the-determinants-of-online-review/9984083238602771)
  is a 2017 meta-analysis reporting that depth, age, reviewer disclosure, and expertise are generally
  associated with helpfulness while other results vary. The accessible record did not expose the
  included-study count or pooled effects, so none are asserted here.
- [Baymard: Shipping costs on product pages](https://baymard.com/blog/show-shipping-costs-on-product-pages),
  [shipping and returns footer links](https://baymard.com/blog/footer-needs-return-shipping-links),
  and [free-shipping banner limits](https://baymard.com/blog/avoid-banners-only-free-shipping)
  document customers seeking delivery and returns information near the decision. Survey and
  usability findings are not universal causal abandonment estimates.
- [Baymard: Product-page suggestions](https://baymard.com/blog/product-page-suggestions) supports
  separating substitutes from complementary products. It does not establish incremental revenue.
- [Fang, Kim, and Chintagunta, “Too Many or Too Few? Information Cues in Recommender Systems and Consequences for Search and Purchase Behavior”](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/00222429251326941)
  reports a 2026 randomized field experiment covering 47,996 customers at one U.S. retailer. One cue
  beat denser alternatives in that retailer's underperforming recommender; this does not permit
  hiding material price or availability information.

## Accessibility and mobile contracts

- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/),
  [Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html),
  [Focus Not Obscured](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html),
  [Target Size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html), and
  [Status Messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) establish the
  accessibility contracts used for narrow reflow, sticky controls, touch targets, and dynamic
  search, filter, variant, and cart updates. Component checks do not prove whole-journey conformance.
- [W3C WAI carousel tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/carousels/) establishes controls,
  keyboard operation, and user control for carousels. It supports retaining equivalent text and
  static paths rather than autoplaying essential commerce content.

## Cart and checkout

- [Baymard: Checkout usability](https://baymard.com/research/checkout-usability) describes a
  long-running program of moderated testing, eye tracking, cross-site benchmarking, and quantitative
  surveys. Its published aggregate abandonment rate and potential-lift estimate are context, not a
  local target or causal guarantee.
- [Baymard: Checkout abandonment reasons](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-checkout-usability-report-and-benchmark)
  reports 42% of a recent U.S. survey sample were browsing or not ready and 17% cited a long or
  complex checkout. It supports segmenting abandonment instead of treating all of it as a defect.
- [Baymard: Make guest checkout prominent](https://baymard.com/blog/make-guest-checkout-prominent)
  reports a 2022 U.S. survey of 4,384 adults in which 24% recalled abandoning at least one cart in
  the prior quarter solely because account creation was required, alongside moderated observations
  of missed guest options. Self-report is not a universal causal effect.
- [Baymard: Delay account creation](https://baymard.com/blog/delayed-account-creation) reports
  distraction and confusion from pre- or mid-checkout enrollment in moderated tests. It supports a
  clear guest path and optional post-purchase enrollment, not a guaranteed loyalty effect.
- [GOV.UK Design System: Addresses](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/addresses/) and
  [text input](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/text-input/) support minimal,
  international, tolerant address entry and precise errors. Public-service guidance is usability
  evidence, not ecommerce lift.
- [WHATWG HTML: Autofill](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/form-control-infrastructure.html#autofill)
  defines browser autofill tokens. Correct tokens aid agents and browsers; they do not replace
  fulfillment validation.
- [FTC: Bringing dark patterns to light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf)
  identifies drip pricing, sneak-into-basket, fake discounts, false scarcity, resetting timers, and
  obstructed cancellation as deceptive design patterns in the U.S. enforcement context.
- [Baymard: Checkout flow and form fields](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)
  reports visible coupon fields interrupting some participants. It supports keeping promo entry
  available but secondary; it does not prove one collapsed design raises conversion everywhere.

## Promotions and scarcity

- [Mathur et al., “Dark Patterns at Scale”](https://bcf.princeton.edu/working-papers/dark-patterns-at-scale-findings-from-a-crawl-of-11k-shopping-websites/)
  reports an automated crawl plus expert review of about 53,000 product pages on about 11,000 sites,
  finding 1,818 dark-pattern instances and 183 sites using deceptive practices. It establishes
  prevalence and taxonomy, not conversion effect.
- [Luguri and Strahilevitz, “Shining a Light on Dark Patterns”](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3431205)
  reports randomized U.S. experiments in which mild and aggressive patterns increased enrollment
  while aggressive patterns also created backlash. It supports rejecting short-term conversion as
  the sole objective; it does not endorse mild manipulation.
- [Cremer and Loebbecke, “Selling goods on e-commerce platforms: The impact of scarcity messages”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1567422321000119)
  reports a two-week observational, cross-sectional, and longitudinal study of roughly 35,000
  printed books with stage-dependent associations between scarcity messages and sales. It supports
  local testing rather than a universal or causal `low stock converts` rule.
- [CMA: Using urgency and price-reduction claims online](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/using-urgency-and-price-reduction-claims-online)
  identifies resetting timers, unsubstantiated stock or demand claims, and fake reference prices as
  risky in the United Kingdom. Other jurisdictions require current review.
- [Google Merchant Center: Sale price effective date](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324460?hl=en)
  specifies explicit start/end timestamps and timezones for Merchant Center sale pricing. It
  supports versioned promotion windows but does not prove a sale is profitable.
- [Zhang et al., “The Long-term and Spillover Effects of Price Promotions on Retailing Platforms”](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v66y2020i6p2589-2609.html)
  reports an Alibaba randomized field experiment involving more than 100 million customers and
  roughly 11,000 retailers: cart discounts increased promoted-day sales but also changed longer-run
  strategic behavior and prices paid. It supports measuring pull-forward and spillover, not copying
  the effect size to another shop.
- [Michael Lewis, “Customer Acquisition Promotions and Customer Asset Value”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkr.43.2.195)
  analyzes customer-level data from a newspaper and online grocer and associates deeper acquisition
  discounts with lower repeat behavior. Its older observational context supports a retention
  guardrail, not a causal universal prohibition.

## Inventory, orders, service, and recovery

- [Shopify Engineering: Scaling inventory reservations](https://shopify.engineering/scaling-inventory-reservations)
  is a provider postmortem describing oversell and undersell from divergent reservation and ledger
  ordering and the use of a short payment hold. It supports one reconcilable lifecycle, not copying
  Shopify's architecture or scale claims.
- [Adobe Commerce: Order status and reservations](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/inventory/basics/order-status)
  and [source selection and reservations](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/inventory/basics/selection-reservations)
  document provider-specific reservation and compensation behavior. They support explicit state
  transitions while leaving schemas, transactions, and locking to the project and database skill.
- [FTC: Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)
  explains U.S. shipping-promise, delay-notice, cancellation, and refund duties within its scope.
  Product and jurisdiction exceptions require current legal review.
- [Baymard: Accounts and self-service](https://baymard.com/blog/new-research-accounts-self-service),
  [order tracking](https://baymard.com/blog/integrate-tracking-info), and
  [returns UX](https://baymard.com/blog/order-returns-ecommerce-ux) report moderated sessions,
  surveys, and cross-site benchmarks supporting accurate status, self-service, and contextual
  support. Their percentages are not universal service targets.
- [FTC: CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
  defines U.S. transactional content narrowly and applies a primary-purpose test to mixed messages.
  Treating cart recovery as commercial is the skill's conservative product inference; current legal
  review owns the conclusion.
- [CTIA: Messaging interoperability principles](https://www.ctia.org/the-wireless-industry/industry-commitments/messaging-interoperability-sms-mms)
  and [2023 update](https://www.ctia.org/news/ctia-updates-messaging-principles-and-best-practices-to-further-protect-consumers-from-unwanted-messages-while-supporting-the-growth-of-messaging)
  require opt-in and effective opt-out as carrier-industry policy. CTIA is not statute; use
  `lead-compliance-gates` and current provider rules for implementation.
- [PCI SSC FAQ 1588](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1588/) describes the PCI DSS 4.0.1 SAQ
  A boundary for embedded versus redirected payment pages and merchant responsibility for script
  attacks. When Laravel and Stripe are in use, implementation belongs in
  `laravel-stripe-payments`; otherwise use the active integration owner and current PCI guidance.
- [Stripe: Dispute lifecycle](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/how-disputes-work),
  [dispute evidence](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/best-practices), and
  [Radar risk evaluation](https://docs.stripe.com/radar/risk-evaluation) document asynchronous
  provider states, deadlines, evidence, false positives, and review. These volatile provider
  contracts support customer-state handoffs. When Laravel and Stripe are in use, implementation
  belongs in `laravel-stripe-payments`; otherwise use the active payment-integration owner.

## Measurement and experimentation

- [Google Analytics: Measure ecommerce](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/ecommerce)
  defines list, product, cart, checkout, purchase, refund, and promotion event shapes with item
  arrays. It supplies a common taxonomy, not application order truth.
- [Google Analytics Data API schema](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/reporting/data/v1/api-schema)
  distinguishes product-list CTR, promotion CTR, purchase-to-view, purchaser, first-time purchaser,
  refund, purchase-revenue, and gross-revenue metrics. It supports keeping intermediate and
  commercial outcomes separate.
- [Google Analytics: Validate ecommerce](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/collection/ga4/validate-ecommerce)
  documents debugging, duplicate transaction identifiers, item requirements, and dual-tagging
  traps. Reconciliation against commerce state remains necessary.
- [Kohavi et al., “Controlled Experiments on the Web: Survey and Practical Guide”](https://robotics.stanford.edu/~ronnyk/2009controlledExperimentsOnTheWebSurvey.pdf)
  synthesizes large-scale web experimentation practice and supports A/A validation, sample and power
  planning, safe ramp, and causal interpretation. It is cross-domain and dates to 2009; current
  platform methods still govern implementation.
- [Johari et al., “Always Valid Inference”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922) presents sequentially
  valid inference for continuously monitored experiments. It supports avoiding repeated fixed-
  horizon peeking or using a supported sequential method.
- [Google Analytics: Attribution settings](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10597962)
  explains that channel eligibility, lookback, and attribution settings redistribute reported
  conversion credit. It does not establish causal lift.
- [Knight and Mitrofanov, “Disclosing Low Product Availability”](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:72:y:2026:i:2:p:1560-1578)
  reports a field experiment involving more than 840,000 Instacart customers where low-availability
  disclosure shifted purchases and improved longer-run outcomes. Its grocery platform context
  supports availability guardrails, not a universal effect size.
- [Gallino and Moreno, “Integration of Online and Offline Channels”](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/09/BOPS.pdf)
  uses a natural experiment at a nationwide retailer and finds buy-online-pickup-in-store shifted
  online and store outcomes differently. It supports evaluating total affected channels; the exact
  result is context-specific.
- [Luo et al., “From Stopping to Shopping”](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormsom/v27y2025i4p1224-1241.html)
  reports a field experiment in which product reminders alone were not significant while selected
  shipping and returns offers late in the funnel affected purchase and profit heterogeneously. It
  supports barrier-specific recovery and holdouts, not automatic coupons.
- [Patel et al., “The impact of free returns on online purchase behavior”](https://doi.org/10.1002/joom.1135)
  uses difference-in-differences around free returns at a Swedish fashion retailer's Danish market.
  Gross margin rose but returns rose enough that estimated short-term cost exceeded the lift. It
  supports evaluating returns and contribution rather than assuming free returns always win.
- [Zhou et al., “How to (and How Not to) Increase Free-Shipping Thresholds”](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6297139.pdf?abstractid=6297139&mirid=1)
  is a 2026 working paper using a natural threshold change at a North American online grocer. It
  reports increased search and abandonment for some shoppers; its preprint and grocery context make
  it a caution, not a universal policy.

## Deliberate exclusions and access limits

- Baymard percentages remain scoped to the cited surveys, tests, or implementation benchmarks. None
  is used as a target CVR or promised lift.
- Google Merchant and Analytics sources are provider contracts, not independent business-outcome
  evidence.
- FTC, CMA, CAN-SPAM, and CTIA materials have different jurisdictions and legal status. The package
  routes current legal conclusions instead of presenting one global ecommerce rule.
- The working papers on filters and free-shipping thresholds are not treated as settled or broadly
  replicated conclusions.
- The full text for some review-helpfulness research and the Google Ads data-driven-attribution help
  page could not be fully verified in the research session. No hidden method count, pooled effect,
  or data-driven-attribution claim from those pages is encoded.
