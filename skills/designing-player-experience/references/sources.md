# Sources

Accessed 12 August 2026. This package independently synthesizes player-experience research, a large
tutorial field experiment, game-usability studies, practitioner literature, and accessibility
guidance. It does not adapt AAAbench. Findings are lenses and bounded evidence, not a universal
formula for enjoyment, onboarding, difficulty, or feedback intensity.

## Experience, usability, and onboarding

- Ryan, Rigby, and Przybylski,
  [“The Motivational Pull of Video Games”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8)
  (2006), reports four studies. Studies 1–3 associate autonomy and competence with outcomes in their
  tested games; Study 4's survey of 730 MMO players found autonomy, competence, and relatedness each
  independently related to enjoyment and intended future play. It supports preserving agency and
  treating experience as multidimensional; the multiplayer relatedness result does not automatically
  generalize to single-player games, and survey association is not a retention recipe or causal claim.
- Andersen et al.,
  [“The Impact of Tutorials on Games of Varying Complexity”](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)
  (CHI 2012), field-tested eight tutorial conditions across three games and more than 45,000 players.
  Effects varied by game complexity; contextual timing helped one complex game, while restricting
  freedom did not show a learnability benefit. This maps directly to the onboarding good/bad pair.
  The games and platform limit transfer.
- Pinelle, Wong, and Stach,
  [“Heuristic Evaluation for Games”](https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357282)
  (CHI 2008), derived usability heuristics from problems reported in 108 PC-game reviews and
  preliminarily evaluated them with five evaluators. It supports state visibility, consistent
  controls, feedback, navigation, and recovery checks; it studies usability, not enjoyment, and its
  PC sample is dated.
- Vanden Abeele et al.,
  [“Development and Validation of the Player Experience Inventory”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.102370),
  developed a multidimensional player-experience scale through expert and player studies. It supports
  measuring named constructs rather than collapsing experience into one “fun” score. Self-report does
  not establish design causality.

## Feedback and accessibility

- Swink et al.,
  [“Game Feel: A Survey”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09201)
  (2020), synthesizes more than 200 academic and practitioner sources into tuning, juicing, and
  streamlining dimensions. It supports distinguishing functional tuning from amplification and
  reducing friction. It is a taxonomy/literature survey, not evidence that more effects improve play;
  this maps to the proportional-feedback pair.
- Microsoft,
  [Xbox Accessibility Guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/guidelines),
  especially [difficulty options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/108),
  [UI navigation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112),
  [time limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/116),
  and [motion settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117),
  supplies community-informed best practices for alternate cues, assists, focus, timing, and motion
  controls. The XAGs explicitly are guidelines, not legal compliance certification.
- Game Accessibility Guidelines,
  [full guideline list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/),
  is a practitioner/community reference for remapping, subtitles, color-independent cues, difficulty,
  input demands, and motion. It supports the accessibility test matrix; affected-player research and
  product/platform requirements still govern implementation.

## Local synthesis limits

- The information contract combines the usability evidence with the XAG task and accessibility
  guidance. No source prescribes one HUD layout or information density.
- The error/recovery and difficulty workflow applies Pinelle et al. and XAGs; the particular burden
  categories are a portable diagnostic vocabulary, not a validated scale.
- The package deliberately keeps commercial retention outside the success definition because the
  tutorial study measured behavior in specific games and motivation research is multidimensional.
