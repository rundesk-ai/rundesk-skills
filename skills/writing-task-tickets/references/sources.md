# Sources

What each source established, and where it shows in the skill. Links verified 2026-10-22.

| Source | Kind | Established | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ron Jeffries, [Essential XP: Card, Conversation, Confirmation](https://ronjeffries.com/xprog/articles/expcardconversationconfirmation/) (2001) | practitioner | The card holds just enough text to identify the requirement; the detail passes in conversation; confirmation is the acceptance test. | A short task carrying outcomes only, the build left to planning; **Done when** as the confirmation. |
| Bill Wake, [INVEST in Good Stories, and SMART Tasks](https://xp123.com/invest-in-good-stories-and-smart-tasks/) (2003) | practitioner | A story is Valuable to the customer, Small, and Testable — "I understand what I want well enough that I could write a test for it" — and is sliced vertically, not by technical layer. | The Problem carries the value; pass-or-fail lines; the split test. |
| Ryan Singer, [Shape Up, ch. 6 "Write the Pitch"](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.5-chapter-06) (Basecamp, 2019) | practitioner | A pitch states the problem as one specific story of why the status quo fails, and names no-gos: anything deliberately excluded. | Problem with its cost; **Not included**; the smaller first version. |
| Mario Maldari, [How to Avoid Ambiguous Requirements in Software Engineering](https://www.jamasoftware.com/blog/avoid-ambiguous-requirements-software-engineering/) (Jama Software) | vendor guidance | Words like "best" or "user-friendly" describe quality without a measurable bound; a term like "immediately" with no defined value makes a requirement unverifiable. | The pass-or-fail rule and its list of vague words. |
| [Ensuring Requirements are Testable in Agile](https://www.pluralsight.com/resources/blog/guides/ensuring-requirements-are-testable-in-agile) (Pluralsight) | vendor guidance | A testable requirement is clear, measurable and complete; a vague term is replaced by what the reader would observe. | Rewriting a vague bar as what someone would see; marking a missing number. |
| Trials of this skill on a lead-generation codebase, with and without it, on two agent providers | recorded experience | Without guidance, agents kept acceptance-criteria mappings and people's names, translated technical requirements one for one, padded lists with unrequested and common-sense lines, and drafted full tasks from vague requests. With it, they still kept some technical guarantees and one provider did not propose splits. | Stakeholder altitude; only what was asked; ask on a vague request; propose a split even when one task was requested. |

The source statements are the sources'; how they map onto this skill's rules is this skill's conclusion.
