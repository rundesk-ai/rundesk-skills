# Logos and brand systems

## Start with recognition, not decoration

Define the audience, brand promise, personality, uses, competitors, and signals the mark must avoid.
Explore distinct concepts in words and rough silhouettes before polishing one. Minimalism, detail,
symmetry, and the golden ratio are not universal quality rules; the mark must fit the intended
identity and remain recognizable in its contexts.

```text
Good: Original symbol-only mark for a neighborhood bakery. Warm, practical, and enduring. One-color
flat construction, few deliberate shapes, strong silhouette, balanced negative space, and generous
clear space. Test at 16, 24, and 48 px and in reverse. No wordmark, mockup, shadow, or stock wheat
badge.

Bad: Perfect symmetrical modern luxury bakery logo, cinematic, ultra-detailed, 8K, with text and
mockups.
```

Use generated marks for exploration. Rebuild the selected direction as controlled vector geometry;
inspect curves, joins, spacing, negative space, and small-size behavior. Typeset the wordmark from
approved fonts and exact copy rather than tracing generated letters.

## Deliver a usable identity

Supply only variants justified by real use:

- primary lockup, symbol, and wordmark where each has a purpose;
- horizontal or stacked alternatives for known spaces;
- one-color, reversed, and approved color versions;
- tested clear space and minimum sizes;
- theme and background guidance, including cases where a different asset is required;
- editable master, production exports, color and type specifications, and usage constraints.

Test the symbol without presentation effects. It must not depend on a mockup, gradient, shadow, or
large canvas to read. Use a grid for construction, then optically balance positive and negative
space. Intentional asymmetry is valid when it strengthens recognition or direction.

## Make the system repeatable

Define roles, not a bag of values: foreground, background, accent, semantic states, and
illustration/icon roles across light and dark themes. Record motif, shape, line, texture, image, and
motion rules with a few approved examples and prohibited transformations.

Do not turn an interface-library icon into a logo. Library terms may forbid trademark use, and a
familiar action metaphor is rarely a distinctive identity on its own.

## Clear rights before adoption

Record the source and license of every reference, font, icon, and component. Confirm modification,
commercial-use, attribution, and redistribution terms. Generated novelty does not prove legal
clearance or exclusive rights.

Before launch, search names and prominent design elements for confusing similarity across relevant
goods, services, and markets. Escalate consequential identity decisions to an accountable human or
qualified legal reviewer; this workflow is not legal advice.
